The Best Base Towns in Himachal Pradesh — And What to Use Each One For

An honest guide to where to stay in Himachal Pradesh, what each town gives you access to, and which type of traveller each one actually suits.

Cover Photo — Himachal Pradesh Towns
Abhinav Chauhan
February 2025
11 min read
Travel Planning

The first decision in planning a trip to Himachal Pradesh is almost always the wrong one: picking a destination from a list of popular places rather than thinking about what kind of trip you actually want. The result is that most people end up in one of three or four heavily visited towns — Manali, Dharamshala, Kasol, Shimla — when the state has dozens of base towns each suited to different purposes, different types of travellers, and different ideas of what a good trip looks like.

This guide covers eight base towns across Himachal Pradesh. For each one: where it is, what altitude it sits at, what it gives you access to, what kind of traveller it suits, and the honest version of its drawbacks. The goal is not to tell you the "best" town — that depends entirely on who you are and what you want — but to give you enough information to choose the right one for your trip.

McLeod Ganj / Dharamshala

Altitude: 1,457m (Dharamshala) / 1,457m (McLeod Ganj)Kangra District

The seat of the Tibetan government-in-exile and home to the Dalai Lama's residence, McLeod Ganj is one of the most visited towns in Himachal Pradesh. The combination of Tibetan culture, a well-developed tourist infrastructure, accessibility from Delhi (12-hour overnight bus), and the dramatic backdrop of the Dhauladhar range makes it a natural starting point for many first-time visitors to the state.

The trekking access from McLeod Ganj is genuinely excellent — Triund, Indrahar Pass, and longer routes into the Chamba district all start nearby. The town itself has restaurants, cafes, yoga studios, and accommodation at every price point. It is also, during peak season, genuinely crowded and commercialised. The version of McLeod Ganj that most people visit — the main market, the popular cafes — is oriented heavily toward tourists.

The honest version: go in October or November to avoid the peak crowds. Stay above McLeod Ganj in Dharamkot or Bhagsu if you want more quiet and better walking access. Give yourself at least four days — the place rewards staying over the tourist circuit version.

Best for: First-time Himachal visitors, cultural tourism, Tibetan Buddhism, trekking access, yoga retreats, accessible infrastructure.

Manali

Altitude: 2,050mKullu District

Manali is the gateway to Lahaul, Spiti, and the Rohtang Pass — which means it handles an enormous volume of transit traffic in addition to its own visitors. Old Manali, a kilometre from the main market, retains some of the character of the original village and is a significantly better place to stay than the main bazaar area. The apple orchards above old Manali are beautiful and largely overlooked by the tourists who arrive by the busload for Rohtang.

The trekking from Manali is world-class: Hampta Pass, Deo Tibba, the Chandrakhani Pass, and the starting point for the Beas Kund trek are all accessible from here. The road north to Lahaul and Spiti starts here. In terms of geographic access to the high Himalayas, Manali is unmatched in Himachal Pradesh.

The honest version: avoid Manali in May and June when the Rohtang day-trip crowds make the town genuinely unpleasant. September is excellent. The market area is overdeveloped and overpriced — Old Manali and Vashisht are where you want to be based.

Best for: Gateway to Spiti and Lahaul, serious trekking, adventure sports, access to high-altitude passes, longer expeditions into the mountains.

Kasol

Altitude: 1,580mParvati Valley, Kullu District

Kasol is the main hub of the Parvati Valley, known for its backpacker culture, Israeli food, and the starting point for the Kheerganga trek. The town is small, the river is beautiful, and the surrounding valley is one of the most scenic in Himachal Pradesh. It is also, during peak season, extremely crowded and quite noisy for a village of its size.

The Parvati Valley above Kasol — Manikaran, Barshaini, Tosh, Pulga, Kalga — is where the real character of this part of Himachal lives. Kasol itself is a transit point and a party town in roughly equal measure. If you want the valley without the noise, stay in Tosh or Kalga instead and come to Kasol only for supplies.

The honest version: Kasol has a reputation that attracts a specific type of visitor, which colours the atmosphere significantly. If that is what you are after, it delivers. If you want quiet and authenticity in the Parvati Valley, stay further up the valley.

Best for: Backpacker travel, Kheerganga trek access, younger travellers, budget accommodation, the Parvati Valley experience.

Jibhi

Altitude: 1,550mTirthan Valley, Kullu District

Jibhi is the best base town in Himachal Pradesh for visitors who want slow travel in a beautiful valley without the infrastructure and crowds of the more famous destinations. The Tirthan River running through the valley is excellent for trout fishing. The trails above Jibhi lead into the Great Himalayan National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and to the Serolsar Lake and Jalori Pass. The town has good guesthouses, a handful of cafes, and the particular quality of a place that has not yet been fully discovered.

Jibhi is two to three hours from the Chandigarh-Manali highway, which keeps the casual day-trip crowds away. The visitors who come here tend to stay for longer and move more slowly. It is, in my experience, one of the best places in Himachal Pradesh to simply be.

Best for: Slow travel, nature, GNHP trekking, families, remote working, anyone wanting the opposite of the tourist circuit.

Kaza

Altitude: 3,800mSpiti Valley

Kaza is the main town of the Spiti Valley and the best base for exploring it. At 3,800 metres it requires acclimatisation but offers access to everything the valley has to offer — Key Monastery, Kibber, Langza, Hikkim, Dhankar, and Pin Valley are all day-trip distance from here. The town has improved its accommodation and dining options significantly in recent years, and while it remains basic by any urban standard, it is comfortable enough for a week-long stay.

The landscape around Kaza is extraordinary. The combination of the high desert, the ancient monasteries, the small farming villages, and the sky at altitude creates an atmosphere unlike anywhere else in India. Give yourself a minimum of five days here.

Best for: Spiti Valley exploration, monastery visits, high-altitude landscape, photographers, anyone doing the Spiti circuit.

Kalpa

Altitude: 2,960mKinnaur District

Kalpa sits above Recong Peo in the Kinnaur district, looking directly across the Sutlej gorge at the Kinner Kailash range — a wall of peaks that rises immediately behind the town with almost theatrical abruptness. The apple orchards of Kinnaur surround it, and the combination of orchard, village, and high mountain backdrop makes it one of the most visually striking towns in the state. It is also significantly less visited than its quality warrants.

The Kinnaur route — Shimla to Recong Peo and on to Spiti — passes through Kalpa, making it a natural stop on the circuit. Allow two nights minimum. The sunrise view of Kinner Kailash from the village temple is one of the finest in Himachal Pradesh and worth planning specifically around.

Best for: Kinnaur culture, apple season (September), the Spiti circuit via Kinnaur, photographers, anyone who wants Himachal without the tourist crowds.

The choice of base town shapes everything else about a trip to Himachal Pradesh. Choose badly and you spend your time in the wrong version of the state. Choose well and the rest tends to take care of itself.

How to choose

The question is not which town is best — it is which town is best for you. A few useful filters:

If you want trekking above 4,000 metres: Manali or Kaza, depending on the specific trek.

If you want cultural depth and history: McLeod Ganj for Tibetan Buddhism, Kaza for the ancient Spiti monasteries, Kalpa for Kinnauri culture.

If you want to slow down and stay somewhere beautiful without the crowds: Jibhi in the Tirthan Valley, or the villages above Kasol in the Parvati Valley.

If it is your first time in Himachal Pradesh: McLeod Ganj in October, with a few nights in Jibhi if you have ten days or more.

If you want the most dramatic landscape: Kaza and the Spiti Valley, by a significant margin.

The mistake most people make is choosing based on what they have heard rather than what they actually want. Himachal Pradesh is large enough and varied enough to offer something genuinely different from the standard tourist circuit. The towns above are starting points. The best way to use them is as bases for staying longer and going slower than your initial itinerary suggests.
