When to Visit Himachal Pradesh — An Honest Month-by-Month Guide

Peak season, off season, and the sweet spots nobody talks about. What the weather actually does, month by month, and what that means for your trip.

Cover Photo — Himachal Pradesh Seasons
Abhinav Chauhan
January 2025
10 min read
Travel Planning

Most travel guides about Himachal Pradesh tell you that the best time to visit is between March and June or September and November. This is broadly true but not particularly useful — it covers eight months of the year and leaves out the nuance that actually matters when you are planning a specific trip to a specific part of a state that spans from subtropical valleys at 400 metres to high-altitude desert at 4,500 metres.

What follows is a month-by-month guide based on years of living and travelling in Himachal Pradesh. It is written for the visitor who wants to understand what they are actually getting into rather than just tick a box on a planning spreadsheet.

One essential caveat before the months: Himachal Pradesh is not a single climate zone. The Kangra and Kullu valleys experience a different climate from Spiti and Lahaul. Dharamshala is very different from Kalpa. Where specific conditions apply to particular regions, the guide says so.

The month-by-month breakdown

January

Cold. Quiet. For the deliberate visitor.

The coldest month in most of Himachal Pradesh. Higher valleys — Spiti, Lahaul, upper Kinnaur — are snow-locked and largely inaccessible. Lower areas like Dharamshala, Kangra, and Palampur are cold but functional. January is excellent for Dharamshala specifically — the town is quiet, the rhododendrons are months away but the Dhauladhar range behind the town is snow-covered and spectacular. Shimla has snow and the associated tourist crowds that come for it. Avoid Shimla in January unless you enjoy traffic jams in the cold.

February

Slowly warming. Still quiet.

February sees the first signs of change at lower altitudes — temperatures begin to rise, days lengthen, and some of the winter-closed guesthouses start reopening in anticipation of March. The higher passes remain closed. February is underrated for the lower Kullu Valley and Kangra — the light is clear, the apple orchards are still bare but beautiful, and the tourist crowds are minimal. Spiti remains inaccessible by road.

March

Excellent. Rhododendrons. Rising temperatures.

One of the best months in the lower and mid-altitude areas. The rhododendrons bloom from late February through March, turning the hillsides above McLeod Ganj and in the Tirthan Valley vivid red. Temperatures are comfortable during the day and cool at night. Trekking trails at lower altitudes — Triund, Kheerganga — are accessible. The Rohtang Pass and Kunzum Pass are still closed, keeping Lahaul and Spiti out of reach until June. March is uncrowded relative to what follows.

April

Peak conditions in the valleys. Crowds beginning.

April is excellent in most of Himachal Pradesh below 3,000 metres. The apple orchards in Kullu and Kinnaur are in blossom — one of the most underappreciated sights in the state. Temperatures are warm in the valleys and comfortable on the trails. This is when organised tourist season begins in earnest, and Manali, Kasol, and Dharamshala start to fill. Book accommodation in advance for any popular destination in April, particularly around Baisakhi.

May

Peak season. Hot in valleys, perfect higher up.

May is when the plains of northern India heat up to forty degrees and the corresponding rush to the hills begins. Shimla and Manali are at maximum capacity. The Rohtang Pass opens in late May, triggering a surge of day-trip traffic from Manali that clogs the road for weeks. If you are going to Manali in May, either arrive mid-week and plan your Rohtang visit carefully or adjust your expectations significantly. Higher-altitude trekking — Hampta Pass, Beas Kund — becomes properly accessible this month. The temperatures at altitude in May are ideal.

June

Spiti opens. The best month for high-altitude travel.

June is when the Kunzum Pass opens and Spiti becomes accessible from the Manali side. This makes June the starting point for the Spiti Valley season. The landscape is extraordinary — snow still on the high peaks, the valleys green with new grass, the roads clear but not yet crowded. The monsoon has not yet arrived in the wetter western parts of Himachal, though Spiti and Lahaul remain rain shadow areas and are largely unaffected by it. June is arguably the single best month for Spiti. Go before the July crowds if you can.

July and August

Monsoon. Avoid lower areas. Spiti remains good.

The monsoon reaches Himachal Pradesh by early July and affects the western and southern parts of the state significantly — Shimla, Dharamshala, Kullu, Mandi, and Kangra all receive heavy rainfall that causes landslides, road closures, and flooding. Trekking in these areas in July and August is possible but conditions are significantly worse than at other times of year. The exception is Spiti and Lahaul, which sit in the rain shadow and receive minimal monsoon rainfall. The Spiti Valley in July and August is actually good — the roads are open, the crowds are there but manageable, and the landscape is at its greenest.

September

The best month. Full stop.

September is the month I recommend to almost everyone who asks. The monsoon retreats, leaving the air clean and the visibility extraordinary. The summer crowds have thinned but the infrastructure is still fully open. Temperatures are ideal across most of the state. The high passes are accessible. The apple harvest in Kinnaur and Kullu is underway, adding an agricultural layer to the landscape that is genuinely beautiful. Trekking conditions are perfect — dry trails, clear skies, the high peaks visible without the summer haze. If you can only visit Himachal Pradesh once and want the best possible version, come in September.

October

Second only to September. Go early in the month.

October is excellent, particularly in the first half. The air remains clear, the temperatures drop into a range that makes trekking comfortable without being cold, and the crowds from summer have largely departed. By late October the high passes — Rohtang, Kunzum — are at risk of early snowfall closure, and Spiti begins to wind down for the season. The trees in the lower Kullu and Tirthan valleys turn in October, and the combination of autumn colour and clear mountain air is as beautiful as anything Himachal Pradesh produces. Go in the first two weeks of October if you want reliability.

November

Transition month. Lower areas good. Higher areas closing.

November is the shoulder month before winter proper. The tourist infrastructure in popular areas begins to close. Guesthouses in Kasol and McLeod Ganj start pulling shutters down. The high passes close for the season. What remains is good — the lower Kangra Valley, Palampur, Dharamshala, and lower Kullu are pleasant in November with mild temperatures and very few tourists. Shimla has its own post-season calm in November that is significantly more appealing than the summer peak version.

December

Winter begins. For the deliberately slow traveller.

December is cold across the state. Snow arrives at higher altitudes and occasionally at mid-altitude towns like Shimla. The Christmas and New Year period brings a specific crowd to Shimla and Manali — young people from Delhi looking for snow and celebration, which is fine if that is what you want and problematic if it is not. Outside the December holiday rush, this is a quiet and rewarding month in the lower valleys for those who have prepared for the cold and want a genuinely unhurried version of Himachal Pradesh.

The honest summary

The two sweet spots are September and early October for general travel, and June specifically for Spiti and Lahaul. March is underrated for the lower valleys. July and August are manageable in Spiti and best avoided elsewhere.

The worst times — and this is the honest version that most travel guides are too cautious to say directly — are the peak summer months of May and June in the most popular destinations (Shimla, Manali, Kasol), when the infrastructure is overwhelmed and the experience of being there is shaped more by crowds than by mountains. Go to the same places in September and they are transformed.

September is the month I recommend to almost everyone. The air is clean, the visibility extraordinary, the crowds thinned, the infrastructure still fully open. It is the best version of Himachal Pradesh.
